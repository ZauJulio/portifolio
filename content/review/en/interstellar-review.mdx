# Interstellar

Few films swing this hard between the vastness of space and the smallness of a single
relationship — and land both. Nolan uses relativity not as a gimmick but as the engine of
its most devastating scene: a father watching decades of his children's lives compress into
the minutes he spends near a black hole.

## What worked

- Hans Zimmer's organ-driven score turns silence and scale into something you feel in your chest.
- The physics of Gargantua, built with Kip Thorne, still looks and holds up years later.
- The docking sequence remains one of the tensest set pieces Nolan has ever shot.

## What didn't

The love-transcends-dimensions beat asks a lot, and the third act leans on it heavily. Whether
it soars or stumbles depends on how much rope you give it — but the emotional core earns the reach.
